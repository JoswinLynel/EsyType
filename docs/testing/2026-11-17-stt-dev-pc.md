# Speech benchmark: Tiny, Base and Small on the development PC

**Date:** 17 November 2026
**Scope (set by the owner):** Whisper Tiny, Base and Small only, using files already downloaded. No other models were run.

## 1. Test conditions

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Machine | i5-11320H (4 cores, 8 threads, AVX2 and AVX-512), 15.8 GB RAM, Intel Iris Xe, no discrete GPU |
| Power | On mains, battery 28% and charging, Windows "Balanced" plan |
| Engine | whisper.cpp 1.8.3 via `whisper-rs` 0.16, CPU only, built with MSVC `/O2` (see section 6) |
| Program | `bench/stt-bench` (in this repository), one process per model so memory figures belong to one model |
| Decoding | Greedy, language fixed to English, no context carried between clips, normal 30 s encoder window unless stated |
| Threads | 4 (physical cores) unless stated |
| Runs | 3 per clip. "Warm" time = mean of runs 2 and 3. Accuracy is scored on run 1 (output was identical across runs). |
| Audio | 10 clips from LibriSpeech test-clean (CC BY 4.0), 2 speakers, about 5, 10, 15, 30 and 60 s each, 238.6 s and 680 reference words in total. Made of whole utterances, so the official transcripts are exact references. |
| Accuracy | Word error rate (WER) after lower-casing, removing punctuation and spelling out numbers (`scripts/wer.py`, self-tested) |

Raw results: `D:\AI Projects\Tools\bench\results\whisper.jsonl` and `summary.json` (outside the repository with the models and audio).

## 3. Accuracy: what the numbers can and cannot say

- 680 reference words means **one word is about 0.15 percentage points**. Base Q5_1 (3.4%) and Small English (4.1 to 4.3%) differ by about six words; that is within noise for a set this small. The ordering Tiny worse than Base is clear (about 15 more errors); Base against Small is not settled.
- Quantisation made no measurable accuracy difference within a model size (Q5_1, Q8 and full precision are within two words of each other).
- Short clips score worse for every model because one error in a 27-word clip is 3.7%.
- LibriSpeech is clean read speech from audiobooks. Real dictation (laptop microphones, names, technical terms, conversational phrasing) will produce higher error rates for all three models, and the gap between them may widen.

## 4. Cold and warm start

| Model | Load from disk | First transcription in a new process (5 s clip) | Warm (5 s clip) |
|---|---|---|---|
| tiny.en Q5_1 | 0.07 s | 2.06 s | 1.27 s |
| base.en Q5_1 | 0.19 s | 2.46 s | 2.34 s |
| small.en Q5_1 | 0.46 s | 7.55 s | 8.22 s |

- Loading is fast (under 1 s for every file) because the files are small, but the model files were almost certainly in Windows' file cache, so these are **not true first-boot figures**. Clearing the cache needs administrator tools and was not done.
- The first transcription in a new process is up to 0.8 s slower than later ones (Tiny). For Base and Small the difference was within run-to-run noise.
- **Design consequence:** load the selected model when EsyType starts and run one silent warm-up pass, so the user's first dictation is not the slow one.

## 5. Thread scaling (base.en Q5_1)

| Threads | 5 s | 10 s | 15 s | 30 s | 60 s | Cores busy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 3.29 | 3.61 | 3.37 | 7.38 | 12.75 | 2.0 |
| 4 | 2.34 | 2.56 | 3.01 | 5.27 | 9.38 | 3.8 |
| 8 | 1.88 | 2.19 | 2.49 | 4.45 | 7.38 | 7.2 |

- Going from 4 to 8 threads (using hyper-threading) is 15 to 21% faster. The architecture's earlier default of "physical cores" is therefore wrong for this CPU.
- **Update, 21 Nov 2026:** measured idle only. With other programs busy, 7 threads became slow and unreliable (median 43 s for 10 s of speech, one failure in six) while 4 stayed at 3.5 s; see `2026-11-21-whisper-threads.md`.
- 2 threads stands in for a low-end dual-core laptop, but only roughly: a real low-end CPU is also slower per core and may lack AVX2.

## 6. Shortened encoder window (experiment, not ready to ship)

whisper.cpp normally processes a fixed 30 s window even for a 5 s clip. Shrinking the window to the clip length:

| Model | 5 s | 10 s | 15 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| tiny.en Q5_1 | 1.27 → 0.27 s | 1.18 → 0.35 s | 1.36 → 0.71 s |
| base.en Q5_1 | 2.34 → 0.34 s | 2.56 → 1.69 s | 3.01 → 1.61 s |
| small.en Q5_1 | 8.22 → 1.12 s | 8.45 → 2.25 s | 8.29 → 3.46 s |

**But it broke 2 of 18 short clips:** the model looped and repeated the whole sentence three times (Tiny on one 5 s clip, Base on one 10 s clip). This is the single largest speed lever found, and the only one that would bring Small within reach on this CPU, but it needs a guard first (detect repetition and fall back to the full window, or a larger margin), then a re-test. It stays off.

## 7. Build finding that affected every number

The Rust `cmake` helper strips `/O2` from MSVC release flags, so whisper.cpp was first built **unoptimised**: Tiny took 8.1 s on a 10 s clip instead of 1.15 s. Fixed by setting `CMAKE_C_FLAGS_RELEASE` and `CMAKE_CXX_FLAGS_RELEASE` in `bench/stt-bench/.cargo/config.toml`. Every result above is from the fixed build. The product build must carry the same setting.

## 8. Limitations

- **One machine.** Nothing here shows how these models behave on 4 GB or 8 GB laptops, older CPUs without AVX2, or faster CPUs. Recommendations for other machines must come from measuring on the user's machine (see the architecture, section 10.3).
- Clean read speech from two speakers; no accents, noise, names or technical vocabulary.
- English only; multilingual Tiny and Base were not tested (not downloaded).
- File-cache state not controlled (section 4).
- Laptop on the Balanced power plan and charging; some run-to-run variation (for example small.en Q8 at 9.05 s for 5 s but 6.87 s for 15 s) is probably power or thermal management.
- Peak RAM is the benchmark process alone; the full app (WebView2 UI) will add to it.
- No GPU acceleration was tried (Vulkan SDK not installed, by the owner's instruction).

## 9. Model choices for the product

One file per tier, chosen from these results:

| Tier | File | Download | Peak RAM (measured) | 10 s clip on this PC | WER here | Why this file |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tiny**: low-resource computers | tiny.en Q5_1 | 32 MB | 245 MB | 1.2 s | 5.6% | Same speed and accuracy as full precision at 40% of the size |
| **Base**: balanced everyday dictation | base.en Q5_1 | 60 MB | 345 MB | 2.6 s | 3.4% | Smallest and most accurate Base file; Q8 and full gained nothing |
| **Small**: more capable computers | small (multilingual) Q5_1 | 190 MB | 601 MB | 8.5 s | 2.9% | Best accuracy of the Small files, same size as the English one, and adds other languages |

**Recommended default: Base (base.en Q5_1).** It meets the 2 to 3 s target for 10 s of speech on an ordinary CPU-only laptop, is clearly more accurate than Tiny, needs about 345 MB of RAM and a 60 MB download. Small was not measurably more accurate on this test and is about three times slower here, so it is only worth offering where the user's own machine proves fast enough.

## 10. Follow-ups found by this benchmark

1. Shortened window with a repetition guard: re-test before considering it (biggest speed gain available).
2. Default thread count to logical processors, not physical cores. (Done; reconsidered on 21 Nov 2026 after testing under load: `2026-11-21-whisper-threads.md` recommends physical cores again, pending the owner.)
3. Load and warm up the model at app start.
4. Test on real 4 GB and 8 GB machines and on a CPU without AVX2 before publishing any recommendation table.
5. A dictation-style test set (laptop microphone, names, technical terms) recorded by the owner.
